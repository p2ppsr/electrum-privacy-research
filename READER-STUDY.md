# Ordinary-reader comprehension protocol — prepared, not conducted

No participants have been recruited, interviewed or tested. The text has not been demonstrated understandable to an average user merely because it is written in plain English.

Recruit adult volunteers with varied wallet experience; record recruitment method, relevant experience and the number completing the exercise. Participation should be voluntary, with no real wallet, transaction, seed, precise IP/location or financial disclosure requested. Assign participant codes; agree retention and quotation consent before recording responses. Pilot wording with a few volunteers before interpreting scores. Use the same materials and order, allow withdrawal, and record all exclusions. This protocol is preparation, not institutional ethics approval or a claim of a representative population sample.

First ask what the person expects a wallet service to learn and whether leaving it stops observation. Then give only [the explainer](EXPLAINER.md) and its diagrams, without researcher coaching. Ask the person to explain the shop story in their own words. Ask:

1. If every customer gets a fresh address, how can one checkout address reveal a wider shop profile?
2. After the shop leaves the old service, what can that service still recognize, and what cannot it automatically recognize?
3. Which participant receives the full query collection? Does every connected service automatically get it?
4. What do TLS and a correctly routed proxy protect? Do either hide the collection from its recipient? What is special about the helper route?
5. Do these experiments prove a named operator is actually surveilling people or that every shared-prefix host has one owner?
6. Do keeping recovery words secret and retaining control of spending prove financial activity stays private?

Predefine the rubric: grouped identifiers plus outside anchor; finite persistent watch set; main/helper/auxiliary role distinction; passive encryption versus authentication versus recipient visibility; capability/assumption versus misconduct/ownership; spending security versus privacy. Score each answer 0 (incorrect), 1 (partial), or 2 (correct with relevant boundary), preserving verbatim responses only with consent. Log completion time, confusing phrases and confidence separately. Compare pre/post misconceptions descriptively, publish the actual denominator and avoid causal/population claims from a small convenience sample.

Revise diagrams/text against observed misunderstandings, retain the earlier materials and version the retest. Independent reviewers should check whether the rubric rewards parroting terms or understanding. Obtain human responses before making a measured user-awareness or comprehension claim.
