# Prior work and contribution

This research does not claim to discover that address-query light clients sacrifice privacy. The upstream [Electrum FAQ, “Does Electrum trust servers?”](https://electrum.readthedocs.io/en/latest/faq.html#does-electrum-trust-servers) already explains that the selected main service can group queried script hashes, connected services see a source IP, unconfirmed information requires trust, and a service can omit history. The [ElectrumX protocol specification](https://electrumx.readthedocs.io/en/latest/protocol-methods.html) documents script-hash subscriptions, histories, transaction retrieval and Merkle inclusion proofs. These are primary sources for the underlying mechanism, not evidence of this particular deployment's retention practices.

The contribution here is a versioned, implementation-specific account of the trust burden: exact ElectrumSVP core/helper destinations and fanout; application associations versus shared infrastructure; source availability and deployment-attestation gaps; official macOS package observations; certificate acceptance in both source and bundled runtimes; and an auditable one-checkout, multiple-payer, post-departure demonstration on a consensus-valid isolated BSV chain. The shop join uses a collection disclosed by the wallet and an outside anchor; it does not need a public-chain common-input heuristic to create the original collection.

The study measures capability under retention and correlation assumptions, not misconduct. It does not estimate how many real users are monitored, prove all hosts share a legal operator, recover unknown people's identities, demonstrate unbounded address derivation, or replace an audit of every wallet/server feature. Prior general privacy warnings do not establish that ordinary users saw or understood the specific helper, persistence and merchant consequences. That is why [the disclosure audit](DISCLOSURE-AUDIT.md), [captured UI](PACKAGED-WALLET.md), and [reader-study protocol](READER-STUDY.md) are separate evidence categories.

Official references were read/refreshed October 29, 2026. Electrum is a related upstream implementation; its documentation is not silently treated as an ElectrumSVP consumer disclosure or a current description of every BSV fork behavior.
